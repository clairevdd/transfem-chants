What this chart does and does not measure

It measures this playlist. Its shape reflects how the playlist was built, which follows collaborations, labels and scenes rather than press coverage, and which therefore reaches recent and online-native artists more easily than older ones. A thin left-hand tail is not a claim that transfeminine music began in the 2010s.

The empty years are shown at their real width for the same reason the grey countries stay on the map: what is missing is not nothing, and hiding it would be the more misleading choice.

1976partialLady on the SubwayBeth Elliott

First publication held: 1976 · known to the year · Indie, folk and traditional music

platform says 2005-01-01, 29 years laterat the latest

The script states, word for word, “That was ‘Lady on the Subway,’ written in 1970 and recorded in 1976”, and introduces it as “a song she wrote in 1970, which you may know was before Olivia Records ever dreamed of a woman-loving-woman song”. Elliott told Doyle it was her way of getting to hear a girl group love song addressed from one woman to another, after the Murmaids’ Popsicles, Icicles. The 1976 recordings circulated the way women’s music circulated then, hand to hand and outside the distribution channels that had closed to her; no catalogue holds them, which is what that exclusion produces rather than evidence against it. Held at 1976 as the earliest known date: she was playing Bay Area coffee houses through the decade and wrote the song in 1970, so a public performance may well be older. Spotify serves the 2005 CD Buried Treasure, twenty-nine years later.

2005unresolvedBallad of the Oklahoma Women's Liberation FrontBeth Elliott

First publication held: 2005-11-27 · known to the day · Indie, folk and traditional music

recorded 1976, 29 years earlierat the latest

Every source agrees the song was recorded in 1976. They disagree on whether it was released. For a real 1976 release: a Wikipedia footnote reads “material Elliott originally recorded and released on a 1976 LP entitled Kid, Have You Rehabilitated Yourself”, and the blog A Gender Variance Who's Who says she put the album out “though the women's music distribution channels were closed to her”. Against: a YouTube notice says “neither came to pass”, and no catalogue knows the record. It is absent from WorldCat, which lists only the self-released 2005 CD, and absent from Discogs, MusicBrainz and the Digital Transgender Archive. Even a poorly distributed pressing would normally leave a Discogs trace. The date held is therefore the CD's, as earliest known, but the case is open: if a copy of the 1976 LP turns up in a collection, the date moves back to 1976.
